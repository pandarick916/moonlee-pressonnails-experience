The first time I trusted adhesive tabs for a date night, one lifted halfway through dinner — and I spent the rest of the night hiding my hand. After maybe fifteen sets and just as many lifts, I finally figured out why they pop, and the brand wasn't the problem.

## Why My Tabs Kept Lifting (It Wasn't the Brand)

What took me too long to figure out: a glue tab is not a one-size sticker. The reason mine kept peeling at the corner was a width mismatch. My thumb bed is on the wider side, and I was slapping on a tab sized for an average nail. The edge had nowhere to grab, so water and soap did the rest.

The AAD notes that clean, dry nails hold adhesive best — oils and moisture are the enemy of any press-on bond.[1](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets/basics/pedicures/manicure-pedicure-safety) Once I understood that, the rest was just prep.

## How I Apply Glue Tabs So They Actually Stay

Five steps. No guessing.

1. **Measure your nail bed width first.** Use a soft ruler and read the millimeters. My thumb runs about 13mm at the wide end, so I grab a tab that covers that, not a generic one.
2. **Match the tab to that width.** If you're between sizes, go bigger — MOONLEE sizes run XS to L with about a 0.5mm margin of error, and the rule is always choose the larger size when in doubt.
3. **Wipe with an alcohol pad.** This is the step people skip. One swipe removes the oil film that makes tabs let go by day two.
4. **Press at a 45-degree angle from the cuticle forward.** Ten seconds of firm, even pressure pushes the air out. I count out loud — sounds silly, works.
5. **Trim any overhang at the edges.** A tiny bit sticking past your nail is where water gets in. A few quick file passes and it's sealed.

## What If You're Worried About Glue Allergies?

Worth taking seriously: some people react to the ingredients in liquid nail adhesives, and the FDA keeps a public page on nail care product safety for exactly this reason.[2](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/nail-care-products) Tabs skip the liquid adhesive entirely, which is why "no-glue press-ons" are the first thing I suggest to anyone who's had a rash from glue. If your skin reacts to anything, stop and talk to a doctor — I'm not one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the best glue tabs for press-on nails if I have wide nail beds?**
Match them to your nail bed width in millimeters — that's the whole answer. My thumb bed is about 13mm wide, so I use an L-size tab (roughly 13.11–14.32mm at the thumb). If a tab is narrower than your nail, the edges lift — that's where the lift starts.

**How do I make no-glue press-on nails last 2 weeks?**
Tabs alone usually get me 5–7 days; for closer to two weeks I use the hybrid method — a thin layer of glue under the tab. Clean, dry nails and a 45-degree press for 10 seconds both matter regardless of which method you use.

**Are glue tabs safe if I've had a nail glue allergy?**
Tabs don't use liquid adhesive, so they sidestep the most common allergen. But check the tab's own materials, and if your skin reacted to glue before, run any new product past a dermatologist first.

**What if my nail beds are small and no tab fits?**
Our smallest size is XS — about 10.5–11.4mm at the thumb and 6.08mm at the pinky. We don't make an XXS. If your pinky is under 6mm, you'll want to trim the tab or go custom; don't force a size that's too big or it'll rock at the edges.

I started keeping a list after that. Here's the checklist I use now, and the red flags that tell me to close the tab.

## Why the Photos Always Look Better Than the Fit

Most sellers optimize for the product photo, not for your actual nail bed width. The result is the same story I hear from friends: the edges float, the corners catch on sweaters, and a week in you're peeling at a lifted sidewall. The worst part isn't the lift — it's that you often can't send them back. You're out twenty bucks and you've learned nothing except that a vague "standard sizes" line was never going to fit you.

## Why It's So Easy to Buy the Wrong Set

Here's why it keeps happening to people who do their research:

**1. Vague size guides.** A single "S / M / L" with no per-finger millimeter measurement tells you nothing about your thumb, which is almost always wider than the rest of your hand. When a nail doesn't match your real width, moisture sneaks into the gap and you get exactly the kind of lift — and, if you're unlucky, the green stain — the American Academy of Dermatology warns about when nails trap moisture against the nail plate[2](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-safety/basic-nail-care-tips).

**2. No third-party review consensus.** Five-star screenshots on a seller's own page tell you the seller knows how to screenshot. What you want is what strangers say on Trustpilot or the Better Business Bureau — unedited, with the complaints visible.

**3. No real return policy.** If a store hides its returns three clicks deep, that's the answer.

**4. Subscription confusion.** Some brands quietly sign you up for a monthly mailer the moment you check out. A "reliable company that mails nails" and a "subscribe and we'll keep mailing you" are two very different things, and the fine print loves to blur them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I buy press-ons for wide nail beds that don't lift?

I look for a brand that publishes per-finger millimeter ranges, not "S/M/L." At MOONLEE our widest L thumb runs 13.11–13.85mm and the middle finger 10.4–11.6mm, so the nail actually covers the bed instead of hanging over the sides. If you see overhang at the corners, the size is wrong, not your hand.

### Are there press-ons for small or XXS nail beds?

Yes — our XS covers thumbs from about 10.5 to 11.4mm, and the smallest pinky in our range is 6.08mm (New Short Squoval XS). If your pinky is under 6mm, I'll be straight with you: file the edges or ask about a custom set rather than forcing a size that sits proud. We don't pretend a single size covers every hand.

### What press-on shape is best for flat nail beds?

Flat beds fight a deep C-curve because the center lifts off the nail. I go for shallow-curve shapes — short round, short oval, or short squoval — and they still come in real widths (about 10.7–13.9mm at the thumb), with a shallow apex that hugs the plate instead of floating. I file the sidewalls to follow the cuticle. Skip anything with a dramatic curve unless you want edges catching on everything.

### How do I tell if a press-on seller is reliable?

A real mm size chart, reviews I can verify on Trustpilot or the BBB, and a return policy I can read before checkout. If any of those is missing, I close the tab.

### What's the difference between mailed subscription nails and a one-time purchase?

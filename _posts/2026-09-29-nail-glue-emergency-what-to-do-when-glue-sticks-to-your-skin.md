A drop of nail glue on a fingertip is a different problem from glue in an eye. Before reaching for remover, check where the adhesive has landed. Do not pull bonded skin apart.

This guide summarizes [Poison Control's cyanoacrylate guidance](https://www.poison.org/articles/super-glue). It is general information, not a substitute for advice about a specific exposure.

## Pause and Choose the Right Route

### Eye or mouth contact: get expert guidance

Do not force bonded eyelids open. Poison Control advises rinsing an affected eye with water for 15 minutes and getting expert guidance. Do not put acetone near the eye or experiment with household oils or tools.

In the US, call Poison Control at [1-800-222-1222](tel:18002221222). Help is free and available 24 hours a day. Outside the US, contact your local poison center or medical service.

### Skin contact: no pulling, no countdown

For skin bonding, Poison Control lists warm soapy water, oils or petroleum jelly, and acetone among possible treatments. These are not interchangeable instructions for every exposure. Treatments can take hours; a 30-second promise is misleading.

If there is pain, irritation, a large spill, or uncertainty about what to use, contact Poison Control for advice about the product and affected area.

## Why Nail Glue Bonds to Skin

Many nail adhesives contain cyanoacrylate, the adhesive family used in super glue. Moisture speeds its polymerization, creating a strong bond. Check your bottle's label rather than assuming every nail adhesive has the same ingredients.

Pulling bonded areas apart can injure the skin. Polymerization also releases heat; Poison Control notes that a large amount on skin can cause burns. Do not treat nail adhesive as a wound-closing product simply because some medical adhesives belong to the same chemical family.

## If Glue Lands on Clothing or Furniture

Separate surface cleanup from care for an exposed person. If glue is on clothing against the skin or there is pain or heat, contact Poison Control instead of treating it as an ordinary laundry stain.

For a spill confined to an object, follow the adhesive manufacturer's cleanup instructions and the surface's care guidance. Do not assume acetone is safe on all fabrics or finishes. This guide does not recommend a universal cotton-swab method or promise that ice will make every spill easy to scrape off.

## Before Your Next Application

**Read before opening.** Check the adhesive label, application directions, and exposure instructions.

**Use a small amount at a time.** Poison Control recommends applying only a small amount rather than squeezing out a large portion.

**Keep it away from your face.** Never open the tube with your mouth. Keep the label visible so it is not confused with eye ointment or another product.

**Store it out of reach.** Keep adhesive and discarded tubes away from children and pets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What can help loosen nail glue on skin?

Poison Control lists warm soapy water, oil or petroleum jelly, and acetone among possible skin-bonding treatments. Do not pull bonded skin apart. Treatment can take hours; ask Poison Control for advice if you are unsure which option is appropriate.

### What should I do if nail glue gets in my eye?

Do not force the eye open or use acetone near it. Poison Control advises rinsing the affected eye with water for 15 minutes and obtaining expert guidance. In the US, call 1-800-222-1222.

### Will glued fingers separate in 30–60 seconds?

There is no reliable 30–60-second deadline. Poison Control notes that skin-bonding treatments can take hours. Do not increase force because a suggested time has passed.

### Can I follow the same removal instructions for clothes and skin?

No. Skin exposure needs a different response from cleaning an object. For glue on clothing against the skin, pain, or heat, contact Poison Control. For an object-only spill, follow the adhesive manufacturer's instructions and the material's care guidance.

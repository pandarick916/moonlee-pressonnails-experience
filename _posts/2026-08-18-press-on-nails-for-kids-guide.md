## What I Was Getting Wrong With Kids' Press-Ons

I was treating her like a small adult. I grabbed the shortest, most neutral set I had, assumed "kid size" just meant shorter, and glued them on. Within two days the corners were lifting because the nail was wider than her actual nail bed, and she'd picked two of them off and lost them under the couch.

The real problem wasn't the glue. It was that her nail beds are a different shape, not just a smaller version of mine.

## Why Store-Bought Kids' Fake Nails Don't Fit

Most press-on brands size by a generic S/M/L scale. A child's nail bed isn't a shrunken adult bed — it's narrower and flatter, and the widths run smaller across the board. [1](https://www.aad.org/public/skin-hair-nails/nail-care/artificial-nails) When the fake nail is even a millimeter too wide, it overhangs the side of the natural nail, catches on everything, and lifts at the corner. That's when she peels it off.

The fix isn't a smaller "kid" label. It's measuring the actual width of each nail in millimeters and matching it. MOONLEE runs XS to L with widths from about 7.5 mm to 13.5 mm per nail — enough to actually fit a child's nail bed.

## How I Finally Got Them To Stay

Three things changed it. First, I measured each of her ten nails with a ruler instead of eyeballing. Second, I used tabs instead of liquid glue — easier to place, and if one lifts she can't yank a glued nail and take a layer of her own with it. Third, I kept the set short. Long nails on a seven-year-old at recess is a recipe for a torn one.

Get the measurement wrong by even 0.5 mm and the rest doesn't matter — measure her exact size first.

## Are Press-On Nails Safe For Kids?

For older kids who won't chew or swallow the pieces, yes, with supervision. The American Academy of Dermatology notes artificial nails carry infection risk if applied over wet or damaged nails, and that matters more for kids who are rougher on their hands. [1](https://www.aad.org/public/skin-hair-nails/nail-care/artificial-nails) Skip them entirely for toddlers — small glued pieces are a choking risk, and their nail beds are still developing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What age can a kid get press-on nails?**
Around 8 is where I'd draw the line — old enough to leave them alone and not swallow a piece. Always supervise the first application.

**How do I size press-ons for small nail beds?**
Measure the widest point of each nail in mm and match to the width chart. Kids usually land in the XS–S range, around 7.5–10 mm per nail.

**Do you make press-ons for wide or small nail beds?**
Yes. MOONLEE sets run XS to L, 7.5 mm to 13.5 mm per nail, sized to your exact mm measurements — not a generic S/M/L guess. Small nail beds are why custom sizing exists.

**How long do kids' press-ons last?**
With tabs and normal play, about a week is realistic. Glue lasts longer but is harder to remove safely, so for kids I'd stay with tabs.

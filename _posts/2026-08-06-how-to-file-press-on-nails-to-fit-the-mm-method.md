I was ten minutes from a work presentation when a press-on I'd just applied lifted at the sidewall — and that's when I started actually measuring. Filing to fit is the step most people skip — and it's why some sets look salon and some look drugstore.

## Why Filing to Fit Matters More Than Filing to Shape

Every nail file review leads with the brand. I'm going to lead with the number. A press-on that's even 1mm wider than your nail bed has nowhere to sit, so it rocks, catches, and lifts at the corner. The fix isn't a better file; it's filing the nail to your width.

The AAD notes that over-filing your natural nail thins it and invites problems, so the goal is to shape the press-on, never grind down your own nail.[1](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/psoriasis/skin-care/nail-care) I don't touch my own nail with the file.

## My 5-Step Filing-to-Fit Method

1. **Prep your natural nail first.** Push back the cuticle, buff the surface dull, and wipe with alcohol. I go through the [full prep routine](https://moonleehome.com/blogs/experience-with-press-on-nails/nail-prep-routine-for-press-ons-the-4-step-guide-that-makes-them-last-2-weeks) before I pick up a file.
2. **Measure your nail bed in mm.** My middle finger runs about 10.5–11.5mm wide, so I know exactly how much press-on I can leave.
3. **File the cuticle edge of the press-on to match your curve.** Short, light strokes from the center out. You're matching the dip near your cuticle, not shaving the whole nail.
4. **Take down the two sidewalls.** The sidewalls are where lift almost always starts. File just the overlap so the press-on sits flush against your skin on both sides.
5. **Seal the edge.** A few gentle passes with a fine file take off the shine and any sharp corner. Run your finger across it — if you feel a ledge, keep going.

## Common Mistakes I Made So You Don't Have To

First: don't file your natural nail to "make room." That thins it and the AAD will tell you the same. Second: don't chase a perfect shape and forget the width — a gorgeous almond that's 2mm too wide will still lift. Width first, shape second.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I file press-on nails to fit my cuticle shape?**
Match the dip near your cuticle with short strokes from center outward. I do this before I even think about the shape. If you feel a ledge when you run your finger across it, keep filing — that ledge is where lift starts.

**How much wider than my nail bed can a press-on be before I should file it?**
Anything over about 0.5mm at the edge is worth filing. Our sizes run XS to L with roughly a 0.5mm margin of error, and the rule is choose the larger size when in doubt — then file the excess down. My wide thumb is about 13.11–14.32mm, and a set in L fits with just a sliver to take off.

**Can I file press-ons if I have small nail beds?**
Yes, and it helps. Our smallest is XS — about 10.5–11.4mm at the thumb and 6.08mm at the pinky. There's no XXS, so if your pinky is under 6mm, file the XS down rather than forcing a size that's too big.

**Should I file my natural nail or the press-on?**
Always the press-on. Filing your own nail to "make room" thins the plate and raises snag and breakage risk. The AAD's guidance is clear: shape the artificial piece, protect the natural one.

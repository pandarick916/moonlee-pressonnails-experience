Ten nails, twenty-four, thirty. The bigger box looks like the better deal until you notice that several of the tips are widths you cannot wear.

Piece count is worth checking, but it answers only one question: how many individual tips are included? It does not tell you how many fit your hands, how many spare thumbnails you will have, or how carefully the set was made.

## 1. Count the pieces, then check the sizing

A ten-piece set can contain different widths selected for each finger. A size label such as S or M may describe a five-finger size combination, not ten identical nails.[1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26618124/) Some sellers also offer custom sizing. Read the size chart for that particular set.

A 24- or 30-piece pack may offer a selection of widths to try, but the total alone does not reveal the size range or how many copies of each width are included. Look for that breakdown before assuming it covers your thumbs and pinkies.

## 2. More choices do not guarantee a fit

Lay the tips out before opening the adhesive. Find a comfortable match for every finger on both hands. If two fingers need the same width, check that the box contains enough of it.

Width is not the whole fit, either.[2](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets) A tip can be the right width yet rock on your nail or pinch because its curve is wrong. A larger assortment gives you options, not a promise that one will suit every nail.

## 3. A spare is useful only if it fits

After choosing your ten tips, look at what remains. Are there matching replacements for the sizes you actually use, or mostly widths you set aside?

A 24-piece box does not automatically contain a second full manicure. A ten-piece set may have no extras in the box, but the seller might offer individual replacements. Check the listing or ask before ordering if that matters to you.

## 4. Compare more than the price per tip

Divide the price by the piece count if you want a quick starting point. Then ask what you are buying: a fitted handmade design, an assortment of sizes, particular materials or finishes, and any included accessories.

A lower cost per supplied tip can be misleading if most of the widths stay in the box. Equally, paying more for ten pieces does not prove they are better made. Compare the product details and the tips you can actually use.

If you track value over time, use your own wear and reuse history. Do not assume the cheaper set lasts fewer days, or that a higher price guarantees longer wear.

## The Bottom Line

Read the count alongside the size chart. For ten pieces, check how the finger sizes are selected. For an assortment, check the widths and duplicates. For either, find out what happens if you need one replacement.

The best-value box is not automatically the largest. It is the one with a design you want, tips that fit and clear information about what is included.

## FAQ

### How many nails come in a press-on set?

You will see packs such as 10, 24 and 30 pieces, among other counts. The number refers to individual nail tips. Check the listing separately for the size assortment and included accessories.

### Is 24 nails enough for two hands?

It can be, provided you can find a suitable tip for every finger. It does not guarantee two full manicures or a spare for each nail, because some widths may not fit you.

### Why are some sets only 10 nails?

They may be supplied as one fitted manicure, with widths selected through a size option or custom measurements. Ten pieces does not mean ten identical widths.

### Do more nails mean better quality?

No. Count tells you quantity. Fit, materials, finish and workmanship need to be assessed separately, and extra pieces are useful only when you can wear them.

### Should I buy a sizing kit first?

A kit for the exact tips you plan to buy can help you check width and curvature. If you measure at home instead, compare all ten fingers with that product’s chart and ask the seller about any uncertain sizes.

I spent a year thinking my press-ons just did not work for me. Every set popped off by day three. I tried six brands of glue. Four brands of tabs. Three dehydrators. Nothing helped. Then I stopped guessing and started troubleshooting like a mechanic. One variable at a time.

The problem was never one thing. It was three things stacked on top of each other, and I kept fixing the wrong thing because I was changing two variables at once. If you are in the same loop, this is the guide I wish I had a year ago.

---

## What Kept Happening

The pattern was always the same. Apply on Sunday night. Feel great. Monday fine. Tuesday a tiny lift at the sidewall of my right index finger. Wednesday the whole nail gone. By Friday I had four missing nails and a set that looked like a before photo.

The worst part was the inconsistency. One set would last ten days and the next would last two, with the same glue, the same tabs, the same prep. I would chase the high of a good set and spiral when the next one failed. I blamed the humidity. I blamed the specific brand of glue. None of it was the full answer.

## Why It Kept Happening

### Root Cause 1: Your glue is old

This is the most overlooked cause of sudden popping off. Nail glue is cyanoacrylate. It absorbs moisture from the air every time you open the bottle. After six to eight weeks the bond strength drops by 30-50%.[1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1163/156856108X305913) The glue looks normal. It smells normal. But it cannot form a full bond anymore. If you have been using the same tube for months and suddenly everything is falling off, this is probably it.

### Root Cause 2: Your nail bed is oily — and you are not prepping for it

Some people can skip dehydrator and get two weeks of wear. If you are not one of those people, skipping dehydrator is why your nails pop off. Oily nail beds form a lipid barrier that prevents cyanoacrylate from bonding to keratin. An alcohol wipe alone is not enough for oily nails — you need a dedicated dehydrator or at minimum two passes of pure acetone before glue. [I dug into the science of oily nail beds](https://moonleehome.com/blogs/experience-with-press-on-nails/oily-nail-beds-your-press-ons-wont-stick-until-you-fix-this) and the fix is simpler than it seems.

### Root Cause 3: The press-on does not fit your nail bed curve

A press-on that is slightly too flat leaves a gap at the sidewall. A press-on that is slightly too curved pinches at the edges and lifts from the center. Both create an entry point for water, air, and mechanical stress. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that ill-fitting artificial nails are the primary cause of lifting and moisture trapping.[2](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets/basics/pedicures/gel-nails-safety) Different brands use different mold curvatures. Width is only half the equation. [Your nail bed type matters more than the brand](https://moonleehome.com/blogs/experience-with-press-on-nails/whats-your-nail-bed-type-why-press-ons-work-for-some-people-but-not-others) of press-on you buy.

## How I Fixed It: The Elimination Protocol

Stop changing three things at once. When a set fails, you cannot tell which variable was the problem if you changed the glue, the brand, AND the prep all at the same time. Fix one thing per test.

### Step 1: Replace your glue. Test for one week.

Before you change anything else, open a fresh tube of nail glue. Write the date on it. Apply one full set. If they stay on for at least a week, your old glue was the problem. If nothing changes, move to step two.

### Step 2: Add a dehydrator step. Test for one week.

After your alcohol wipe, apply a nail dehydrator or a second pass of pure acetone. Let it evaporate completely before applying glue. Do this with fresh glue from step one. If your wear time doubles, your nail beds are oily and you now have your answer forever.

### Step 3: Check your nail bed curve against your press-on shape.

Hold a press-on up against your natural nail in good light. Look at the gap at the sidewall. If there is daylight between them, the curve does not match. You need a flatter or more curved press-on. [Measure your nail width first](https://moonleehome.com/pages/press-on-nail-size-chart), then pay attention to curvature. A nail that fits in width but not in curve will always lift.

The elimination protocol is boring. One variable at a time. One week per test. But it is the only way to know which of the three causes is actually your problem.

## What a complete press-on kit actually contains

There is no industry standard here, which is the root of most disappointment. Two sets at the same price can contain wildly different amounts of stuff.

**Quick answer — what comes in a fake nail kit?** A complete press-on kit contains 24 to 30 nails across 10 to 16 sizes, plus adhesive. According to Allure's 2026 press-on review, its top-rated $20 kit also includes glue, cuticle oil, a prep pad, a cuticle pusher, and a two-in-one file and buffer.[1](https://www.allure.com/gallery/best-press-on-nails)

Here is every component you might find and whether it is worth having:

- **The nails (24–30 per set).** Non-negotiable — but what matters is not the count, it is the **size spread**. A 30-nail set in 15 sizes is far more useful than a 30-nail set in 10 sizes, because you only need ten nails — you need the right ten.
- **Liquid glue.** Worth having, but kit glue is usually the cheapest component. I have had kit glue fail at day four and standalone glue hold for three weeks. If you wear press-ons often, buy adhesive separately.
- **Adhesive tabs.** Included in maybe half of kits. Useful for short wear and for testing fit before you commit.
- **Prep pad or alcohol wipe.** Small item, disproportionate impact. This is the dehydration step, and skipping it is the single biggest cause of early lifting.
- **Mini file and buffer.** Genuinely useful — you will file the nail to fit, not just your natural nail.
- **Cuticle pusher.** Useful. A rubber or wooden pusher is better than metal for home use.
- **Cuticle oil.** Nice to have for after removal — not before application, where it will ruin your bond.
- **Cuticle trimmer or nipper.** Skip it — see below.

## The one thing in most kits you should not use

Cuticle nippers show up in a surprising number of nail kits, positioned as the professional touch. Dermatologists disagree.

**Quick answer — should you cut your cuticles before applying fake nails?** No. The American Academy of Dermatology states that cuticles protect nails and surrounding skin from infection, and that trimming them makes it easier for bacteria to get in — and that cut cuticles grow back rough, creating a trimming cycle.[2](https://www.aad.org/public/skin-hair-nails/nail-care/artificial-nails)

Push them back with a rubber pusher instead. You get the same clean cuticle line for application purposes, without opening a route for infection right before you seal the nail under a plastic tip for two weeks. The trimmer in the box is there because it looks professional in a product photo.

## The component nobody lists: sizing

Every kit sells you objects. None of them sell you the thing that determines whether the set works, which is whether any of those 30 nails match your actual nail widths.

A kit with 30 nails in 10 sizes gives you three duplicates of each size. If your index finger falls between size 4 and size 5, you have no option — you are wearing something slightly wrong on that finger for two weeks, and it will lift first. A kit with 16 sizes gives you the resolution to actually match.

Before buying any kit, measure your nails once and write the numbers down. It takes about thirty seconds a hand using [the credit-card measuring method](https://moonleehome.com/blogs/experience-with-press-on-nails/measure-finger-size-30-seconds-credit-card-tool), and after that you can evaluate any kit's size chart against real millimeters instead of hoping. ## Using the kit: the order that makes a difference

Most kits include an instruction card with four steps. Here is the order that actually holds — especially if you are looking for a **press-on nail kit for beginners** that lasts more than a few days.

1. **Size all ten dry, before any adhesive.** Lay them out on the table in finger order. Every nail should sit inside your sidewalls with no overhang onto skin.
2. **Trim your natural nails short and file them flat.** Length underneath is leverage against the bond.
3. **Push cuticles back.** Do not cut them.
4. **Buff lightly — one or two passes, 220 grit.** You are removing shine, not thinning the nail.
5. **Wipe with 70% isopropyl alcohol and wait two full minutes.** This is the step in the kit that costs almost nothing and does the most.
6. **Apply adhesive, place at the cuticle line, roll down, hold 30 seconds.** Thirty. Under real pressure.

If you want more depth on any of these, [the complete beginner's guide](https://moonleehome.com/blogs/experience-with-press-on-nails/press-on-nails-a-to-z-the-complete-beginners-guide-2026) covers the whole process, and [the sandwich method tutorial](https://moonleehome.com/blogs/experience-with-press-on-nails/sandwich-method-press-on-nails-tabs-glue-tutorial) covers combining tabs and glue for longer hold.

## FAQ

**What comes in a fake nail kit?**
A complete press-on kit contains 24 to 30 nails across 10 to 16 sizes, plus adhesive. Better kits add a prep pad, a mini file and buffer, a cuticle pusher, and cuticle oil. The prep pad is the most underrated item — skipping the dehydration step is the single biggest cause of early lifting.

**Are all-in-one nail kits worth it?**
For your first set, yes — you get tools you will keep for years. After that, buying nails and adhesive separately usually costs less per set, because you stop re-purchasing files, pushers, and buffers you already own. Kit glue is typically the weakest component.

**How many nails should a kit include?**
Count matters less than size spread. Thirty nails in 16 sizes is more useful than 30 nails in 10 sizes, because you only wear ten — you need the ten that match your nail widths. Check the size range before the nail count.

**Should I use the cuticle trimmer that came in my nail kit?**
No. The American Academy of Dermatology advises against cutting cuticles, because they protect the nail and surrounding skin from infection, and cut cuticles grow back rough. Push them back with a rubber or wooden pusher instead.

**What is the difference between a press-on kit and an acrylic nail kit?**
A press-on kit gives you finished nails you apply with adhesive in 10 to 20 minutes with no skill required. An acrylic kit gives you monomer, powder, forms, and a brush to build nails yourself — typically 90 minutes per set and weeks of practice to do well.

**Do press-on kits include sizes for flat or wide nail beds?**
Most kits are one-size-fits-most, which is exactly why they lift. MOONLEE kits run XS to L with widths from about 7.5 mm to 13.5 mm per finger, and if your beds are flat or wide, size up one and use gel tabs instead of glue — tabs conform to the surface where glue lifts.

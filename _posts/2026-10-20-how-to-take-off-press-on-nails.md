A lifting corner is not an invitation to pull the whole tip off. Before removing press-ons, identify the adhesive you used and find its removal directions. A method suitable for one set may not be suitable for another.

This guide helps you choose the correct instructions and avoid force. It does not promise damage-free removal or provide a universal ten-minute soaking recipe.

## Start Here: Which Attachment System Did You Use?

### Adhesive tabs

Read the tab manufacturer's removal method. If it specifies water, oil, or a dedicated remover, follow that method and its timing. Do not assume every tab can be peeled off dry or that a partially loose tip is ready to pull.

### Liquid nail glue

Use the remover and procedure specified for the glue, and check compatibility with the tip. Warm water is not a universal substitute. Do not choose acetone solely because another guide used it for a different adhesive.

### UV-cured or mixed systems

Use the system's own removal directions. Tabs under glue do not establish an easy-peel setup. If you cannot identify the products or their instructions, ask the manufacturer or a qualified nail professional before improvising.

## A No-Force Removal Checklist

### 01 · Gather the correct supplies

Read the adhesive and remover labels first. Check the specified application, exposure time, and whether the remover affects the nail tips or their finish.

### 02 · Work somewhere stable

Use a well-lit, stationary workspace and follow the products' handling directions. Do not rush removal while travelling or preparing to leave.

### 03 · Follow the procedure, not a countdown

Use the manufacturer's method. If a tip resists, do not pull harder because ten minutes have passed. Do not wedge a stick, blade, or another nail underneath to pry it free.

### 04 · Pause if there is a problem

If you feel pain or see irritation or damage, stop rather than increasing force or experimenting with more products. Seek appropriate professional advice.

## Acetone, Water, and Oil Are Not Interchangeable

Whether a remover is appropriate depends on the actual attachment system and product instructions. “Gentle” is not a substitute for compatibility. A method that preserves a reusable tip may not be the method required to release its adhesive.

Oil is not an instant repair for a nail plate and does not guarantee that splitting will stop. After removal, follow ordinary care directions and give yourself time to assess the natural nail before applying another set.

## Before You Apply Another Set

✓ Follow the product instructions for removing remaining adhesive.

✓ Do not aggressively scrape or buff to make the surface look clean.

✓ Check the natural nail and surrounding skin before reapplying.

✓ Skip reapplication over painful, irritated, or damaged areas.

✓ Keep the adhesive's instructions so the next removal is planned from the start.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use acetone to remove press-on nails?

Check the adhesive's removal instructions and compatibility with the tips. Acetone is not a universal requirement or a universal substitute for a specified remover. Follow the actual product directions rather than another set's method.

### How long should I soak before the nails release?

There is no universal ten-minute rule. Follow the specified method and timing for your adhesive. If a tip resists, do not force it off because a suggested time has elapsed.

### Can I slide a cuticle stick under a lifting tip?

Do not use a tool to pry a resisting tip off. A lifted corner does not establish that the rest of the bond has released. Follow the attachment system's removal procedure and seek help if you cannot remove it without force.

### Why are my natural nails thin or peeling after removal?

This guide cannot determine the cause from that description. There may be more than one contributing factor, and recovery cannot be promised after one set. Pause reapplication and seek professional advice if the problem persists or there is pain or irritation.

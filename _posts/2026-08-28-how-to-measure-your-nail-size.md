I measured my nails wrong for years. Not slightly wrong. Wrong enough that half my press-ons lifted at the edges by day two, and I blamed the glue.

The glue was never the problem. The ruler was — or rather, the fact that I wasn't using one. If you've been guessing your size or trusting the S/M/L on the box, here's the honest version of **how to measure your nail size**, the way that actually keeps press-ons on.

**Quick answer —** Measure the widest part of your nail bed, just below the free edge, in millimeters. Not the length, not the tip, and not the S/M/L on the box. Most people land somewhere in the 8–14 mm range across their ten fingers, and almost every finger needs a different size.

## What I Was Doing Wrong

I'd eyeball it. Hold the fake nail up to my finger, squint, decide "close enough." Sometimes I'd use the little paper sizing card that came in the kit — and I'd measure the wrong part of the nail. Then I'd pick a size that looked right and wonder why the edges caught on everything I touched.

Here's the specific moment it clicked. I was on a work call, and my right ring finger's press-on just... tilted. Not fell off. Tilted, like a loose tooth. Because it was half a millimeter too wide and the glue couldn't hold an edge that wasn't flush. I'd measured my nail length, not the **width of a fingernail**. That's the number that actually matters.

## Why It Kept Happening (The Root Cause)

Your nail bed is wider than you think, and it's not the same on every finger. The thumb is the widest. The pinky is narrow. A press-on that's even half a millimeter off sits on a tilt — one edge presses down, the other floats, moisture gets in, and the whole thing lifts. The American Academy of Dermatology points out that a nail that doesn't sit flush traps moisture and bacteria underneath, which is exactly what leads to the green staining people panic about.[1](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets)

Most press-on kits use S/M/L or a 10-12 size range. That's fine if your nails are average. Mine aren't. Neither are yours, probably. The fix isn't a better guess. It's measuring in millimeters and matching to a real **nail size chart**.

How to Measure Your Nail Size

1

Measure the widest part, not the tip

The number you need is the width of your nail bed at its widest point — that's just below the free edge, where the nail meets skin. Why there? Because that's where the press-on sits. Measure the tip and you'll pick a size that's too small at the base. Take a flexible ruler or a piece of tape, mark the two widest edges, and read the millimeters. That's your nail size.

2

Work in millimeters, not S/M/L

S/M/L means nothing across brands. One brand's small is another brand's medium. Millimeters don't lie. Most people land in the 8–14 mm range across their ten fingers — and here's the thing nobody warns you about: **nail sizes for fingers** are different for almost every single digit. My thumb is 15 mm. My pinky is 8. I need different sizes, not one size for the whole hand. If you've ever wondered why the set that fit your index finger popped off your thumb, that's it.

3

Match to a real size chart and go edge-to-edge

Once you have your millimeters, compare them to the brand's sizing chart — and if a brand doesn't publish one, skip it. A press-on that fits means flush edge-to-edge with no gap and no overhang. Overhang catches on hair and zippers. A gap invites water. When you get this right, the nail sits like it grew there, and it stays for the full one-to-two weeks instead of three days.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I measure my nail size at home?

Measure the widest part of your nail bed — just below the free edge — in millimeters. A flexible ruler or a piece of tape marked at both edges works. Compare that number to the brand's sizing chart.

### What is the widest part of my nail to measure?

The nail bed at its widest point, just below where the nail grows off the finger. Not the tip, not the length. Width is the number that decides whether the press-on sits flush.

### Do I use millimeters or S/M/L for press-on nails?

Millimeters. S/M/L isn't consistent across brands, but millimeters are. Most fingers run 8-14 mm wide, and almost everyone needs a different size on different fingers.

### What if my nail beds are narrow or wide?

Then generic S/M/L sizing will fight you. Narrow beds need sets with extra-small options; wide beds need sets with a real size chart. Skip any brand that doesn't publish one.

"Free custom sizing" sounds like the answer. It's usually a promise with no measurement behind it — and a promise can't keep your nails on. A measured fit can.

Here's the contrarian take the ads won't give you: **custom fit press on nails** are often less precise than a well-measured standard set. The word "custom" does a lot of heavy lifting in this category, and most of it is marketing, not sizing.

## Why "Custom" Is Usually a Looser Fit, Not a Tighter One

Read the fine print and "custom sizing" usually means you pick from six shapes and four lengths. That's not custom. That's a dropdown menu. You're still eyeballing your own nail and hoping you picked right — except now you've paid more for the privilege of guessing.

Real custom — the kind where a technician actually measures your nail bed and cuts to it — requires you to measure accurately and submit it. And here's the problem nobody admits: most people measure their own nails wrong. They measure the tip instead of the widest part. They round up. They guess. So a "custom" set built from a bad measurement is worse than a standard set that was sized properly.

The promise targets your anxiety, not your nail bed. It says "this will finally fit" without ever asking for the one number that decides fit.

## What I Do Instead: Measure, Then Match

The number that matters is the width of your nail bed in millimeters. That's it. Not your length, not your shape preference, not whether the brand calls it custom. Width at the widest point, just below the free edge. Get that right and any set sits edge-to-edge with no gap and no overhang.

That's a **measured fit**. And it's the thing **custom press on nails** brands skip when they hand you a shape-and-length menu. The American Academy of Dermatology makes the stakes plain: a nail that doesn't sit flush traps moisture underneath, and trapped moisture is where green staining and lifting start.[1](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets) Fit isn't a luxury feature. It's the whole game.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between custom fit and measured fit?

Custom fit often means picking from a shape-and-length menu, which is still guessing. Measured fit means your nail bed width in millimeters is matched to a real size. One asks you to guess. The other asks you to measure.

### Is custom sizing worth it for press-on nails?

Usually not, unless a technician is actually measuring your nail bed and cutting to it. Most "custom sizing" is a dropdown menu you pick from — and if you measure yourself wrong, you've paid more for a worse fit.

### How does a measured fit work?

You measure the widest part of each nail bed in millimeters, then match to a brand's size chart. Edge-to-edge fit with no gap and no overhang is what keeps the nail on and keeps moisture out.

### Which is better for wide or flat nail beds?

A measured fit, every time. Wide and flat beds are exactly where generic S/M/L and "custom" menus fail. You need a real width number and a size chart that covers it — skip any brand that doesn't publish one.

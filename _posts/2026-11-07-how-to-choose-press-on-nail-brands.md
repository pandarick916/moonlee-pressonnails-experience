You have two tabs open, two designs you like and no clear way to tell which set will fit. The photos show the artwork. Now look for the details that help you choose the tips underneath it.

Start with sizing, length and profile. Then check what comes in the box, whether replacements are available and what happens if the set is not right for you. These six checks help you compare listings without pretending there is one best brand for every hand.

## Look Beyond the Top-Down Photo

A top view can show the design beautifully while leaving the underside curve and thickness unclear. A familiar size letter is not enough either:[1](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets) M in one range may describe a different combination of widths in another.

You do not need to buy several full sets just to ask those questions. Look for the chart, ask for another photo or check whether a sizing kit is available for the exact tips you want.

## 1. Measurements, Not Just Letters

Check the chart for the design, shape and length you plan to buy. If it lists millimetres, also read the measuring method: a curved surface measurement and a straight-across measurement are not automatically interchangeable.

Measure all ten fingers rather than copying one hand to the other. If the listing provides only a thumb-based size guide, ask how the remaining fingers are matched. Measurements narrow the choice; they do not guarantee the underside curve will suit your nails.

## 2. What “Short” Actually Measures

Short describes a range, not a fixed amount of free edge on every hand. The same tip may extend differently depending on the length of your natural nail bed.

Look for the tip length and how it was measured. A comparison photo can help you judge the look, but a number is easier to compare with a set you already know.

## 3. A View of the Profile

A side view shows the lengthwise profile. An end view shows the transverse curve, while an underside view can help you understand how the tip is shaped where it meets your nail.

Do not choose solely by a visible apex or assume that more curve means better wear.[2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38367770/) A tip that rocks, pinches or needs force to sit down deserves another fit check. If photos leave that unclear, a sizing kit made from the exact tips can be useful.

## 4. Piece Count and Size Assortment

A ten-piece set may contain different widths selected for each finger, through a preset combination or custom sizing. It does not mean ten identical widths.

A 24- or 30-piece box may offer more widths to try, but it does not promise a match for every finger or a second full manicure. Check how many copies of each width are included, especially if several fingers need the same size.

## 5. Can You Replace One Tip?

If losing one tip would stop you wearing the set, ask about replacements before ordering. Check whether the seller can match the design and size, what it costs and how long it takes.

A replacement service may be useful to you, but its absence does not establish poor workmanship. The point is to know your options rather than discover them after a tip goes missing.

## 6. Read the Return Conditions

Look for the actual policy rather than relying on a short promise near the buy button. Check which circumstances it covers. Ask how to contact the seller, and whether opened, tried-on or custom-sized sets carry conditions.

Do not assume “damaged on arrival” also covers “the shape does not fit my nail.” If you are unsure, ask before applying anything. Clear answers help you decide what purchase risk you are comfortable with; this checklist is not a legal assessment of a policy.

## Then Compare the Price

Compare what the total includes: design work, materials, tip quantity, useful sizes, adhesive, accessories, delivery and any replacement costs. The lowest price per supplied tip is not necessarily the lowest price for the tips you can wear.

Cost per wear can be useful once you have your own experience with a set. Before buying, avoid treating advertised wear time as a certainty or assuming an expensive set will outlast a cheaper one.

Some of these questions the listing can answer. The rest you have to put to the seller, and the answer is worth waiting for. Choose the design you like once you know what you are buying.

## FAQ

### How do I choose a press-on brand when I cannot try the nails on?

Compare the exact product’s size chart, measuring method, length and profile views. Ask about a sizing kit if curvature or width is uncertain, then check the included pieces, replacement options and return conditions.

### Is a more expensive press-on brand always better?

No. Compare the design, materials, workmanship, useful sizes and what the price includes. Higher price alone does not establish better fit or longer wear.

### Should my first set be a multi-size pack?

It can give you several widths to try, but check the assortment first. A fitted ten-piece set or an exact-tip sizing kit may also be suitable. Piece count alone cannot tell you what will fit.

### Can measuring guarantee that my first set fits?

No. Measurements help narrow the choice, but curvature, measuring technique and the product’s sizing system also matter. Dry-fit every tip before applying adhesive and do not force a pinching or rocking tip into place.

### Does the brand name matter?

It can help you find designs or service you already know. For a new set, still check the exact product details rather than assuming every shape from that brand has the same fit.

You find an M that fits, order an M in another design, and the new tips pinch or hang over the sides. The letter stayed the same. The tips did not.

Keep your measurements, but check the new chart. A familiar size label is a useful note about one set, not a shortcut for every set you buy.

## A Size Letter Is Not a Measurement

XS, S, M and L are labels within a seller’s range. They may describe a combination of widths for five fingers, not a single width repeated across the set. Other packs use numbered tips or a mixed assortment instead.

When changing brands, shapes or lengths, compare the measurements for the exact tips you want. Do not assume the same letter carries the same dimensions.

## Changing Length Does Not Automatically Mean Sizing Up

A longer tip is not automatically wider. Different shapes and lengths may use different moulds, but the direction of any size change has to come from the product chart or an actual fitting, not a rule that long always means large.

> A familiar letter is a starting point. The new product’s measurements and fit are the check.

Width is only part of it.[1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26618124/) A tip can match a number yet pinch or rock because its curve is different. If a set sits badly, check the shape as well as the letter instead of assuming the adhesive is the problem.

## Keep Measurements for Both Hands

**Measure all ten fingers.[2](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets)** Use the method specified by the seller, including whether the measurement follows the nail’s curve or is taken straight across. Numbers obtained by different methods are not automatically comparable.

**Use the exact chart.** Note the brand, shape and length beside the measurements. A thumb-only table can help narrow a choice, but it cannot confirm every finger’s fit.

**Check uncertain sizes before buying.** A sizing kit made from the exact tips can help with width and curvature. If you fall between entries, ask the seller what they recommend rather than automatically choosing a larger tip and filing heavily.

For a numerical example, a 12.2 mm tip is 0.4 mm narrower than a 12.6 mm nail measured the same way; a 13.0 mm tip is 0.4 mm wider. Neither number alone establishes a comfortable fit, and the thumb entries above should not be treated as universal tip dimensions.

## FAQ

### Why can my press-on size change between brands?

Size labels describe a seller’s own range and may use different width combinations. Compare the measurements and measuring method for the exact product instead of treating M or S as a universal size.

### Should I size up or down if I am between two sizes?

Check the seller’s fitting advice or try a sizing kit for those tips. A tip should not pinch or need force to sit down. File only if the maker permits it, and do not use heavy filing to rescue a poor fit.

### Does the length of the nail change my size?

It may come with a different mould or chart, but longer does not automatically mean wider. Check the listed dimensions and curvature for the length and shape you want.

### Can my hands need different sizes?

Yes, corresponding fingers need not match. Measure both hands rather than adding a fixed allowance to your dominant hand or assuming one side must be wider.

### Can I buy a multipack and find tips that fit?

A mixed-size pack gives you options, but the piece count does not guarantee a match for every finger. Check the size range, duplicate quantities and curvature, then dry-fit before applying adhesive.

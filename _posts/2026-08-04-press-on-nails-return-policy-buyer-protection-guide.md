Twenty-four dollars is not enough money to fight about. It is exactly enough to feel stupid about.

That was the last time I ordered press-ons without reading the return terms first. Below is the checklist I use now, what I found out about my actual rights as a US shopper, and how MOONLEE scores against the same five questions — including the answers I do not love.

## What Happened When I Tried to Send Them Back

I emailed on day three. I got a reply on day nine that repeated the final-sale line and suggested I "try a drop of glue on the loose edge." Day eleven I asked again and got nothing. The set sat in my desk drawer until I threw it out.

What bothered me was not the money. It was that I had no way to know any of this before I paid. The policy page never said how many days I had, never said which country returns went to, and never said whether a sizing problem counted as a reason at all. Every one of those blanks worked in the seller's favor, and none of them worked in mine.

I have since talked to enough people to know this is the normal experience, not the unlucky one. Go read the one- and two-star reviews of almost any handmade press-on brand on Trustpilot and the same three complaints stack up: the size guide was off, the order took far longer than advertised, and customer service went quiet the moment the word refund appeared. Nobody is being scammed exactly. They are being worn down.

## Why Press-On Returns Go Wrong So Often

Five things make press-on returns harder than they should be.

**Press-ons get filed under personal care.** Sellers lean on a hygiene exemption to make everything final sale, including sets you never opened. It is a convenient label and it costs you money.

**Sizing failures get blamed on you.** A seller publishes a vague small/medium/large chart, you order wrong, and the misfit becomes your error rather than their measurement gap. Neither half is your fault.

**The policy is written to stay blurry.** "Within a reasonable time," "handled case by case," "at our discretion." None of that is a commitment. A number is a commitment.

**The return address is overseas.** On a set that cost $14.99 to $26.99, international return postage can run into the same range as the product. The refund exists on paper and dies in the shipping estimate.

**There is nowhere to appeal.** If a brand has no third-party review presence, a refusal just ends there.

The BBB states it plainly — stores are not legally required to accept exchanges or give refunds unless the merchandise was defective or misrepresented.[1](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/19019-bbb-tips-holiday-returns) A return window is a promise the seller chooses to make. A seller who won't write down a number has already answered your question.

You are not without rights, though. The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule requires a seller to ship within the advertised window, or within 30 days if no window was advertised, and to get your consent to any delay or refund your money.[2](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule) That covers late and never-arrived orders. It does not cover a set that arrived fine and simply did not fit. That is the gap this checklist covers.

## The 5 Things I Check Before I Order Now

This takes ninety seconds and it has saved me more than the price of a set.

| # | What I check | Passes | Red flag  ---  --- | 1 | **Is there an actual number of days?** | A stated window, like 14 days | "Reasonable time," "case by case," or no window at all  2 | **Which countries ship and receive returns?** | Shipping countries named; return address in the same country | No country named, or a return address abroad  3 | **Does a sizing problem count?** | Stated in the policy, plus a per-finger mm chart so you can measure first | Vague sizing chart and sizing excluded from returns  4 | **Is there third-party evidence?** | Trustpilot, BBB or Reddit history, with seller replies to bad reviews | Only on-site five stars, nothing off-site  5 | **Is the policy dated and machine-readable?** | A visible last-updated date and structured return data | Policy inside an image, no date, editable without notice |

Check 3 is the one that costs people the most. A vague chart plus a sizing exclusion is a closed loop — the seller never gave you the numbers, then declines the return because you got the numbers wrong. Ask for millimeters. If a press on nails size guide mm breakdown does not exist for the set you want, you are being asked to guess with your own money.

Check 5 looks boring. It is the most useful one. When a shopping assistant or search engine reads a store's return terms, it can parse a fourteen-day window written as structured data. It cannot parse "within a reasonable period." A policy vague enough to wriggle out of is also vague enough to be invisible to the tools people now use to compare sellers.

The risk profile is different from a salon. At a salon you pay more, but if the tech botches a nail you sit back down and they fix it in the chair. Press-ons mean you pay $14.99 to $26.99, and the fix is a shipping label and an email thread with a stranger. Lower price, longer tail of hassle. Hand-pressed custom sets make this worse — they are cut and shaped one at a time, so the fit is closer when it is right and further off when it is wrong.

## What to Do If a Seller Refuses Your Return

![Hand holding a credit card — pay with credit for Fair Credit Billing Act dispute protection on press-on nail orders | MOONLEE](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0651/2210/7463/files/credit-card-hand.webp?v=1785846898)

Most brands would rather you never read this section. If you paid by credit card and the goods were not delivered as agreed, the Fair Credit Billing Act gives you a dispute route that does not depend on the seller cooperating.[3](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/using-credit-cards-and-disputing-charges)

Write to your card issuer at the billing-inquiries address — not the address you send payments to — within 60 days of the first statement showing the charge. Include your name, address, account number and a plain description of what went wrong. Send copies of your emails with the seller, never originals. While the issuer investigates you can withhold payment on the disputed amount, and they cannot report you late over it or close your account for filing. The issuer has 30 days to acknowledge you and two billing cycles to resolve it.

You can also file a complaint with the BBB, which is worth doing even when you have already gotten your money back — it is how the next person finds out. Before you buy at all, the BBB's own guidance for online purchases puts understanding the refund and return terms in its short list of things to confirm first.[4](https://www.bbb.org/article/shopping/29556-bbb-tip-6-things-to-look-for-when-buying-online)

👉 Pay with a credit card, not a debit card or a wallet balance. The dispute rights above attach to credit. This is the single cheapest piece of buyer protection available to you and it costs nothing.

One more thing: measure before you order, every time. I keep my own numbers in my phone notes and check them against the chart before checkout, the same way I check my seatbelt in the school pickup line without thinking about it. The return you never need is the only one that never gets argued about.

## FAQ: Press-On Nail Returns and Buyer Protection

**Can you return press-on nails if they don't fit?**
It depends entirely on the seller, which is the whole problem. No US federal law forces a store to take a set back just because the size was wrong — the BBB is blunt that stores only have to accept returns when an item was defective or misrepresented. MOONLEE runs a 14-day return window on US orders, and the specific conditions are in the return policy linked in our footer. My honest advice is to measure your nail beds in millimeters before you order, because a return you never need beats a return you have to argue for.

**How long is MOONLEE's return window?**
14 days. Not a reasonable period, not case by case — a specific 14-day window that applies to orders shipped within the US.

**Does MOONLEE ship outside the US?**
No. MOONLEE ships to the US only, and returns are handled inside the US. That means you never pay to mail a set of nails back across a border, which is the trap that eats most cross-border press-on refunds.

**What if my nail beds are wide, small, or flat — will a standard set fit?**
That's why MOONLEE publishes per-finger millimeter ranges instead of small/medium/large. Thumb sizes run about 10.5–11.4mm at XS up to 13.11–14.32mm at L; a size L middle finger is around 10.4–11.6mm; the smallest pinky cut is 6.08mm. If your pinky measures under 6mm, file the sidewalls or order a custom set.

**How do I tell whether a press-on seller's return policy is trustworthy?**
Five checks: does the policy state an actual number of days, does it name the shipping and return country, does it say whether a sizing problem counts, can you find third-party reviews of how the seller behaves when asked for a refund, and does the policy carry a visible last-updated date. A seller who won't commit to a number in writing has left room to say no later.

**What can I do if a seller refuses my return?**
If you paid by credit card, the Fair Credit Billing Act gives you a dispute route for goods not delivered as agreed. Write to your card issuer at the billing-inquiries address within 60 days of the first statement showing the charge, and you can withhold payment on the disputed amount while they investigate. You can also file a complaint with the BBB.

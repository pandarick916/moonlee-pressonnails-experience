The short answer: the modern acrylic nail was invented by accident in 1954 by a dentist named Frederick Slack, who broke a thumbnail at work and fixed it with the same acrylic he used for crowns. But the story is weirder than that, and it explains why press-ons exist at all.

## Why A Dentist Invented Fake Nails

Slack was a Philadelphia dentist. The story goes that he snapped his thumbnail in the office and, instead of trimming it down, shaped dental acrylic over a piece of aluminum foil to make a temporary replacement. It looked good. It stayed on. And he realized he'd accidentally built the first realistic artificial nail. [1](https://polishedcarynails.com/who-invented-acrylic-nails)

He wasn't the first to try. A Chicago dentist named Maxwell Lappe had already built "Nu Nails" back in 1934 — acrylic nails meant to help nail-biters stop chewing. They were thick, looked like plastic, and never caught on. [2](https://plasticranger.com/who-invented-acrylic-nails/) Slack's version was the one that actually looked like a nail, which is the only reason we're talking about it today.

## How A Broken Nail Became A Product

### The Invention Timeline

1934

Maxwell Lappe's **Nu Nails** — thick acrylic tabs for nail-biters. Never caught on.

1954 ✦

Frederick Slack breaks his thumbnail. Fixes it with dental acrylic. **The modern artificial nail is born.**

1957

Slack brothers patent the technique. **Patti Nails** launches — the first commercial nail product.

1960s

Adhesive-backed **press-on nails** arrive. The salon look becomes a kitchen-table DIY.

1978

Stuart Nordstrom launches **CND**. The liquid-and-powder system most salons still use today.

Slack and his brother Tom patented the technique and started a company called Patti Nails in 1957. Later the family founded NSI (Nail Systems International), which still supplies professionals. In the 1970s, another dentist, Stuart Nordstrom, built the liquid-and-powder system most salons still use and launched CND (Creative Nail Design) in 1978. [3](https://plasticranger.com/who-started-acrylic-nails/) So the entire industry traces back to people whose day job was teeth.

One detail worth knowing: the early acrylics used MMA, which made nails hard but wrecked the natural nail underneath. It was banned in 1974 in favor of EMA, the safer formula that's standard today. [1](https://polishedcarynails.com/who-invented-acrylic-nails)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who invented fake nails?**
The modern acrylic nail was invented in 1954 by dentist Frederick Slack, who built a replacement for his broken thumbnail from dental acrylic. Earlier attempts date to 1934.

**When were press-on nails invented?**
Adhesive press-on nails arrived in the 1960s, about a decade after salon acrylics, to make the look doable at home.

**Do you make press-ons for wide or small nail beds?**
Yes. MOONLEE sets run XS to L, 7.5 mm to 13.5 mm per nail, sized to your exact mm measurements — because S/M/L sizing is how you end up with a set that lifts by day two.

**Are acrylic and press-on nails the same thing?**
No. Acrylics are sculpted in a salon from liquid and powder; press-ons are pre-made nails you glue or tab onto your own. Same origin story, different application.
